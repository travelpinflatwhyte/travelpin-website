Christmas in Goa, beyond the parties

How Goa really celebrates Christmas and New Year

By Satish, founder of Travelpin. A six-minute read.

Every December, half of India seems to land in Goa. Most of it heads straight for the beach clubs.

That's fine. But while the parties go on in Baga and Anjuna, the villages are doing something older. Paper stars glow outside homes and along the streets. Kitchens have been frying sweets for a week. Churches fill at midnight. And on New Year's Eve, a stuffed old man sits on a chair by the road, waiting to be burnt.

If you're going to Goa this December, here's how to see that side of it too: what to eat, what not to miss, and what to plan around.

What to eat

Goan Christmas is a food festival first. The centre of it is kuswar, a platter of homemade sweets that families make in the days before Christmas and share with neighbours, friends and colleagues. If a Goan friend hands you a plate, that's the greeting.

You might find:

Bebinca
The queen of the platter: a layered cake, built one thin layer at a time
Neureos
Fried pastries stuffed with coconut, cashews, raisins and cardamom
Kulkuls (kidyo)
Little curled bites of fried dough, flavoured with coconut and cardamom
Dodol
A dark, chewy sweet of rice and local jaggery
Doce
Chickpea and coconut fudge
Perada
A tangy-sweet guava cheese
Bolinhas
Coconut and semolina cookies
Marzipan
Often shaped into tiny fruits, flowers and stars
Kormolas
Coconut pastries shaped like flower buds
Baath
A moist coconut and semolina cake, sometimes topped with a pastry lattice

You don't need a Goan friend to try them. In December, bakeries and home bakers across Goa sell kuswar boxes, and they make the best edible souvenir you can carry home.

Beyond the sweets. Christmas lunch is a family feast. If a restaurant offers a Christmas menu, look for sorpotel and sannas, or a pork vindaloo, the dishes Goan families cook for the day.

What not to miss

December in Goa is a month of feasts, not just one night. Here's the calendar for 2026.

  1. 3 December

    Feast of St Francis Xavier

    Old Goa

    Goa's biggest feast. Pilgrims arrive from across India, some on foot. Go early; it gets very crowded.

  2. 8 December

    Feast of the Immaculate Conception

    Panaji, at the church on the hill

    The white church with the zigzag stairs, Goa's most photographed, at its most festive.

  3. 13–20 December

    Serendipity Arts Festival

    Venues across Panaji

    A week of art, music, theatre and food, spread across the capital.

  4. All month

    Stars and cribs

    Villages across Goa

    Paper stars outside homes and nativity cribs by the roadside. Betalbatim in South Goa is known for its cribs.

  5. 24 December, late

    Midnight Mass

    Churches across Goa, including the Basilica of Bom Jesus

    Goa's Christmas starts in church. Visitors are welcome; dress modestly and arrive early.

  6. 24 and 31 December

    Village dances

    Parish and community halls

    Goa's other party: live bands, families and dancing until dawn.

  7. 31 December, midnight

    Burning the Old Man

    Roadsides in almost every village

    Boys build an effigy of the old year from hay and old clothes, then burn it at midnight to leave the year's troubles behind.

Walk Fontainhas at dusk. Panaji's old Latin quarter, with its painted houses and narrow lanes, looks its best with December lights.

Go south for a quieter Christmas. If the north feels like one long party, the villages of South Goa keep Christmas closer to home.

What to plan around

Two airports, far apart. Goa has two: Dabolim in the south and Mopa in the north. Check which one your flight uses before you book a stay. The drive between them is long, and longer in December traffic.

Book early. Flights and stays for the last two weeks of December fill up and get expensive. If your dates are fixed, book as soon as you can.

December traffic. The roads to the northern beaches crawl, especially in the evenings and around New Year's Eve. Leave much earlier than you think, particularly on the way to the airport.

Respect the feasts. Churches are places of worship first. Dress modestly, keep your phone quiet during Mass, and ask before photographing people.

New Year's Day is slow. Many places open late on 1 January. Plan an easy day.
