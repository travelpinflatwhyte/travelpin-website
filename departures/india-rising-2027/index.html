India's rising places for 2027

Up-and-coming places in India for 2027, and the evidence behind each one.

By Satish, founder of Travelpin. Last checked 9 November 2026.

Goa's international flights landing in the far north, 2025

The new airport at Mopa moved the front door of Goa to the top of the map. The beaches near it are rising.

Every year brings a list of "the next big places". Most of them are guesses. This one isn't: every place here earns its spot with something you can check, whether a new airport, a new railway, visitor or permit numbers that went up, or a rule that changed.

Some are inside places you already know, like the beaches at Goa's northern tip or the hills of Kerala. Some are new on the map in 2027. A few are places people say are coming, which may not be ready yet. I've said so where that's the case, and I've left out two popular claims that didn't hold up.

If you only read one thing

  1. Goa's front door moved north. Most international flights now land at Mopa, and the beaches of Pernem are rising. Morjim is also a turtle beach from November to February.
  2. Kerala's hills overtook Kochi. Idukki, home to Munnar and Thekkady, became Kerala's most-visited district for travellers from within India in 2025.
  3. Adi Kailash is the fastest riser here. This year's permits passed all of last year's within 40 days.
  4. 2027 has one fixed date to plan around: the Kumbh at Nashik and Trimbakeshwar, with its main bathing day on 31 August 2027.
  5. Don't plan around Sikkim's railway or the Zojila tunnel yet. Both have dates that have moved before.

What the labels mean

  • RISING
    Visitor numbers or access changed recently, and it's still quieter than its famous neighbour.
  • ROSE, DIPPED
    It grew fast, then fell back. Still far busier than before.
  • STILL QUIET
    Protected or fragile. Go gently, or not at all at certain times.
  • OPENING UP
    Rules or access changed in the last year or so.
  • DATED
    A fixed event in 2027.
  • NEWLY LISTED
    A new World Heritage listing, with no visitor figures yet.
  • NOT YET
    Announced, but the date has slipped before.

Goa: the beaches at the top of the map

Morjim, Mandrem, Arambol and Querim

The famous ones: Baga, Calangute and Candolim. Rising: the beaches of Pernem, Goa's northernmost area.

  • The airport moved closer. The new Manohar International Airport at Mopa is in Pernem. In 2025 it handled 1,141 of Goa's 1,784 scheduled international flights, about 64%, according to Goa Tourism. Many visitors from abroad now land at the north end of the state.
  • More beach shacks were licensed there. Goa's tourism department added two shacks each at Morjim, Mandrem, Arambol and Querim.
  • Goa overall is busier than ever: 1.08 crore (10.8 million) visitors in 2025, up 34% on 2019, according to Goa Tourism.

RISING Quieter than Baga, but not empty in December.

  • Morjim is a turtle beach. Olive ridley turtles nest there from November to February, the same months as peak season. The Forest Department relocates eggs to protect them, and beach businesses are asked to switch off lights and clear furniture at night. Keep your torch off and give marked nests a wide berth.
  • Mopa is about 50km from Dabolim, Goa's older airport near Vasco. If you're staying in the north, check which airport your flight uses.

Galgibaga, and why it should stay quiet

Galgibaga, in Canacona, is a turtle-nesting beach with a Forest Department turtle centre and a nursery. Tourism there is limited and mostly on private land.

STILL QUIET Visit for a day, keep to the rules at the turtle centre, and stay in Palolem or Agonda.

Inland Goa, beyond the beaches

Goa's government is pushing visitors inland, away from the crowded coast. Its ecotourism plan, set out in March 2026, includes:

  • White-water rafting on the Mhadei river, in Sattari, during the monsoon.
  • Treks to Dudhsagar Falls, run by the state tourism corporation in the monsoon.
  • Eco-cottages at Selaulim, already built.
  • Planned: a wellness centre at Anjunem in Keri, an eco-forest retreat at Chapoli Dam in Canacona, and a women-led jaggery village at Cotigao. No opening dates yet.

RISING As official policy. Best in the monsoon, from June to September, which is also Goa's quietest season.

Kerala: the hills and the north

The famous ones: Kochi, and the beaches of Kovalam and Varkala. Rising: the hills of Idukki, including Munnar, Thekkady and Vagamon; and Kannur, in the far north.

  • Idukki overtook Ernakulam in 2025, with 46.8 lakh domestic visits against 44.3 lakh, to become Kerala's most-visited district for travellers from within India. In 2024, Ernakulam led by 43.3 lakh to 38.3 lakh.
  • Kannur passed 10 lakh domestic visits, and its visitors from abroad grew by about half, from a small base (2,649 to 4,021).
  • For visitors from abroad, the backwaters grew fastest: Alappuzha up 48% and Kottayam, home to Kumarakom, up 36%. Thiruvananthapuram, home to Kovalam and Varkala, fell 3%.

RISING Idukki is busy at weekends and in the Christmas holidays; go midweek.

Kannur is the home of Theyyam, the ritual performance held in village shrines from mid-October to late May. Visitors are welcome to watch; Kerala Tourism publishes a Theyyam calendar. Muzhappilangad, Kerala's only drive-in beach, is about 17km from Kannur's airport.

Beyond the Char Dham: Adi Kailash and Om Parvat

The famous ones: Kedarnath, Badrinath and the Char Dham. Rising: Adi Kailash and Om Parvat, high in Pithoragarh district near the Tibet border.

  • Permits are rising fast. Visitors need an Inner Line Permit. In 2025, 36,526 were issued for the whole season. In 2026, that total was passed within 40 days of the season opening on 1 May.
  • Homestays have opened along the route, which the district magistrate credits for the growth.

RISING The season runs from May to November, with a break in July and August for the monsoon.

Go well: it's high altitude near an international border. Inner Line Permits are handled by the sub-divisional magistrate's office in Dharchula. The road beyond Dharchula is prone to landslides: in September 2026, a bridge on the route washed away twice in two weeks and delayed the autumn season. Rules for foreign nationals may differ, so check with the Pithoragarh district administration first.

Beyond Manali: Lahaul, through the Atal Tunnel

The famous one: Manali. Rose fast, then dipped: Lahaul, on the far side of the Atal Tunnel, including Sissu and Keylong.

  • The tunnel cut the journey. Manali to Sissu now takes about 45 minutes instead of six hours, and Manali to Keylong about an hour and a half instead of eight.
  • Then it dipped. In 2025, domestic visitors fell by more than half. Reports point to weather, road conditions and people choosing other places. Visitors from abroad held steady.

ROSE, DIPPED Even after the dip, Lahaul gets nearly three times as many visitors as in 2018.

Locals have raised concerns about litter and traffic in a fragile valley. Go midweek, stay a night or two rather than doing a day trip, and take your rubbish back with you.

Opening up

Srinagar by train

Since 7 June 2025, Vande Bharat trains have run from Katra to Srinagar in about three hours, across the Chenab bridge, 359 metres above the river. The road takes six to seven hours.

OPENING UP For the first time, you can reach Srinagar by train, changing at Katra. Book early; seats sold out in the first weeks.

Go well: check your government's travel advice for Jammu and Kashmir before you book; advice for some areas differs by country.

Lakshadweep: easier to get in

In April 2026, Lakshadweep dropped two of its hardest entry rules. You no longer need a local sponsor or a police clearance certificate from your home state; the Lakshadweep Police now does the security check after you apply.

OPENING UP You still need an entry permit.

Apply at least 14 days ahead, list every island you'll visit, and book flights only once the permit arrives. You enter and leave through Kavaratti or Agatti.

Mizoram: on the railway for the first time

On 13 September 2025, Mizoram joined India's railway network with the Bairabi to Sairang line near Aizawl, with new trains to Delhi, Guwahati and Kolkata.

OPENING UP Visitors need a temporary Inner Line Permit to enter Mizoram.

About that 145% figure: Mizoram's widely reported 145% rise in visitors covers 2023 to 2024, before the railway opened. The railway's own effect hasn't been measured yet.

New on the map in 2027

Nashik and Trimbakeshwar: the Simhastha Kumbh Mela

The Kumbh at Nashik and Trimbakeshwar comes round once every 12 years. The Maharashtra government has set the dates:

BATHING DAYNASHIKTRIMBAKESHWAR
First Amrit SnanMon 2 Aug 2027Mon 2 Aug 2027
Second, the main dayTue 31 Aug 2027Tue 31 Aug 2027
ThirdSat 11 Sep 2027Sun 12 Sep 2027

DATED The biggest gathering Nashik will see in 12 years.

Book a room as early as you can, and expect heavy crowds and security on bathing days. If you want the atmosphere without the crush, go on a weekday between bathing days.

The Maratha forts: India's newest World Heritage listing

In July 2025, twelve forts became a single UNESCO World Heritage Site, the Maratha Military Landscapes of India: Salher, Shivneri, Lohgad, Raigad, Rajgad, Pratapgad, Panhala, Vijaydurg, Sindhudurg, Suvarnadurg and Khanderi in Maharashtra, and Gingee in Tamil Nadu.

NEWLY LISTED No visitor figures since the listing yet, so a place to watch rather than a proven riser.

The best time for the hill forts is after the monsoon, from October to February. Sindhudurg, Suvarnadurg and Khanderi are sea forts, reached by boat.

Not yet: don't plan around these dates

  • NOT YET Sikkim's first railway, Sivok to Rangpo. Sikkim's chief minister has said it will open in 2027. It was first due in 2022.
  • NOT YET The Zojila tunnel to Ladakh, for year-round road access from Srinagar. Reports range from early 2027 to 2030.
  • NOT YET India's first bullet train, from Surat to Bilimora in Gujarat, has a target of 15 August 2027, with the full Mumbai–Ahmedabad line by 2029. It's a ride, not a destination yet.

What I left out, and why

  • Gurez, in Kashmir. Often called the next big valley. Visitors rose to 1.1 lakh in 2024, then fell to about 55,000 in 2025, according to figures given to the Jammu and Kashmir assembly. Not rising.
  • Tawang, after the Sela tunnel. There are reports of record crowds and traffic jams at Sela Pass, but no published visitor numbers yet.
  • Rajasthan's lesser-known districts. The published figures give visitor totals but no growth rates, so there's nothing to show which are rising.
