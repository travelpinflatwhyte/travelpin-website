How the subscription works, start to finish

Following Laya, a brand-new user, from install to a paid annual plan — every state she passes through and what flips it.

Stage 0 Free

Installs & signs in with Apple

The app calls GET /v1/me/subscription. The server — the source of truth — answers: tier: free · isPro: false · 0/5 trips. Laya is now a known Free user.

She just uses the app…
Stage 1 Free

The free travel journal

She adds trips, drops pins, browses her Basecamp stats — all free. The app is fully useful as a journal by design; that's the retention & reviews engine. Every add-trip sheet quietly refreshes her usage count.

She adds her 6th trip
Stage 2 Paywall

She hits the cap

Before saving, the app checks canCreateOwnedTrip → 5 < 5 = false, so it shows the paywall instead. Backup: even if that passed, the server rejects with 403 SUBSCRIPTION_REQUIRED and the same paywall appears.

Phase 2 (not built yet): the wall also appears contextually — when she turns on live flight tracking, adds a Live Activity, or pairs the Watch. That's the "Free = journal, Pro = assistant" reframe.
Annual is pre-selected · she taps Start Free Trial

The paywall & her choice

All options visible at once:

Monthly $4.99/mo

Stage 4 Free trial · $0 now

Purchase — trial starts, no charge

Apple returns a verified transaction. The app sends the signed receipt to POST /v1/subscriptions/app-store/sync, re-refreshes → isPro: true, and finishes the transaction. The paywall closes, her 6th trip saves, Pro lights up. Apple schedules the first charge for day 7.

Day 7 arrives · she didn't cancel
Stage 5 Pro · paid

The trial converts

Apple charges $29.99 and auto-renews yearly. A background Transaction.updates listener catches the renewal, re-syncs to your server, and keeps her Pro — nothing for Laya to do.

If she cancels during the trial: she stays Pro until day 7, then reverts to Free. Trips over the cap remain viewable; she just can't create new ones past 5.

The other path — a one-off Week Pass

For Laya-the-once-a-year-traveller who doesn't want to subscribe.

She taps the Week Pass link and pays $2.99 once — full Pro for 7 days, unlimited trips in that window. Because it's non-renewing, StoreKit doesn't track its expiry, so your backend computes the 7-day window from the purchase date and flips her back to Free when it lapses. Since Pro is the live-feature layer (not a permanent trip-unlock), when it expires she simply loses live tracking — nothing kept. That's what closes the old $0.99 loophole.

Free → pays $2.99 → Week Pass · Pro for 7 days → server expiry → Back to Free

Who owns what

The one-line mental model behind all of the above.

StoreKit / Apple

The money & the receipt. Charges the card, runs the trial, handles renewals, refunds, and cancellation. Your app never touches payment details.

Your server

The source of truth. Given Apple's signed receipt, it decides isPro and the limits, and computes non-renewing expiry.

The app

The reader. Fetches status, gates the UI, and shows the paywall at the right moment. UX only — never authoritative.

New phone & managing it

Two flows that need no re-payment or custom billing screens.

Restore

New iPhone → Restore runs AppStore.sync(), re-syncs her purchase to your server, and Pro returns. No second charge.

Manage / cancel

The footer's Manage Subscription deep-links to Apple's subscriptions page. Apple owns cancellation & refunds end to end.

⚠︎ Two honest caveats before you test

  • Freemium is OFF in production today (FREEMIUM_ENABLED). With it off, everyone is treated as Pro and no paywall triggers at all — flip it on to see Laya's journey.
  • Two pieces are Phase 2, not built yet: the server-side Week-Pass expiry, and the feature-based gating + contextual paywalls. Today the only gate is the 5-trip cap.
