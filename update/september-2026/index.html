Disruption Assistant Released

On cancellation, diversion, or significant delay, the app surfaces four answers in one place: what happened, what to do next, what compensation may apply, and how to rebook. No paywall on this feature.

Scope
Uses existing Cirium monitoring. Classifies disruption, shows a step playbook, and computes an advisory rights band by regulator (not by airline).
Offline
Playbook and rights rules ship in the binary — usable without network (important at the gate / abroad).
Delivery
Status transition push opens the trip into the Disruption UI.
Rebooking
Pre-filled Google Flights search (same route/date) in-app. Avoids a paid flight-search API for a low-frequency event. Rights shown before the rebook action so travellers see free-reaccommodation options first.
Engineering cost
Swift on-device engine · Cirium · APNs · Google Flights deep link.
Risks fixed
Diversions treated as their own case (not collapsed into “delayed”). Copy is advisory (“may be owed”) — Travelpin does not assert entitlement.

Partnership evaluation · AirHelp

Travelpin explains rights; it does not operate a claims practice. I am evaluating whether to integrate with AirHelp for optional claim filing on eligible international routes.

AirHelp
Flight-compensation company (since 2013). Checks eligibility under passenger-rights law, files and negotiates with airlines, escalates to legal action when required.
Model
No-win, no-fee: ~35% of recovered compensation; ~+15% if litigation is required. No fee if unsuccessful. Also sells AirHelp+ membership (waives claim fees; not an insurance product).
Fit
Best where statutory cash is material (EU/UK 261 and similar). Weak fit for India domestic DGCA claims (lower amounts, administrative process) — those stay in-app, free, self-serve.
Economics
A referral would monetise a high-intent disruption moment without Travelpin building claims operations. Not committed; economics and UX conflict (commission vs free airline claim) need to be designed in-product if I proceed.
Status
Under evaluation — no agreement

Destination Explore Released

Per-destination place shortlists (see / outdoors / eat / coffee), tied to the traveller’s trip context (arrival/departure, stay, phase of trip). Cap ~20 places per category after merge.

Data
Hybrid: Travelpin curated seeds + Google Places + Gemini. Cached ~30 days so cost scales with cities refreshed, not with opens.
Hubs
Multi-area destinations: Costa Rica (San José hub → La Fortuna, Manuel Antonio, Monteverde, Corcovado); Goa (statewide default + Panaji and area chips); India hubs (Mumbai, Delhi, Kochi, Bengaluru).
Product detail
Opening hours in destination timezone; Best vs Nearby; optional map (off by default).
Edge
Uses trip + stay + flight timing already in Travelpin. Generic maps products have richer POI databases but not this trip state.
Curation process
ResearchDestination shortlists from travel research and known traveller guides — owned names, not open-web scrapes.
ClassifyEvery place lands in one of four buckets: see · outdoors · eat · coffee.
StructureHub catalogue for multi-area destinations, plus related-area chips travellers can switch between.
PublishSeed → hybrid merge with Google + AI (cap ~20) → ratings / place IDs attached.
Challenge
How to present the best curated locations without drowning the traveller — four clear buckets, hub vs neighbourhood defaults, and a hard cap after merge so the shortlist stays usable.
Stack
NestJS + Postgres cache · curated/hub JSON · Google Places · Gemini · SwiftUI client.

Manual day planning Released

Travellers build their own day itineraries in the trip: add places from Explore or search, assign them to days, reorder stops, and adjust the plan as the trip unfolds.

What it is
The trip Plan / itinerary surface — day-by-day list the traveller owns and edits.
From stay
For each outing, the plan shows road distance and drive time from the stay (MapKit directions), then drive legs between consecutive stops on that day. Long hops are flagged so a day does not silently stack unrealistic travel.
Works with
Destination Explore shortlists (add a place into a day), stay as home base until another stay is added, and existing trip structure.
Status
Released in shipping builds. This is the planning path travellers use today.
Built · not released

Plan My Day AI-assisted — not released

AI-assisted generation of a proposed day from curated places, stay location, flight times, and opening hours. On-device planning engine plus a Gemini path I am iterating on. UI gated off in shipping builds.

Field tests (Goa, Madrid) failed quality bars:

  • Duplicate landmarks across days (per-day planning with no trip-level memory)
  • Arrival-day proposal too far from the stay
  • Later days under-filled after early days consumed strong areas
  • Hard failure when no hotel was set (Madrid)
Decision
Do not release AI-assisted proposals that can be confidently wrong on hours or sequencing — trust loss is expensive. Manual day planning remains the shipped path.
Path to release
Trip-level allocation (cluster areas → assign to days → sequence within day → lock accepted stops), then re-test on real itineraries.
Stack
On-device Swift planner · MapKit travel-time prefetch (throttled) · Gemini for AI day proposals (cached in Postgres) — still under evaluation against the field failures above.
